At the end of each cycle, the company archives the old quarter and plans the new one. Because **OKRs don't carry over between cycles**, the rollover is a deliberate sequence — here it is, stage by stage.

## Stage 1 — Close out the old cycle

Before archiving, spend one last week in review:

- Managers mark finished key results **Done** and let final check-ins land.
- Owners skim the Company Dashboard — **Pace**, per-team progress, and the AI themes — as the quarter's record.

## Stage 2 — Start the new cycle (owner)

In **Admin → OKR Cycle**, click **Start new cycle** and confirm. The old cycle is archived; the new one starts at Week 1 with a fresh auto-generated label and **no objectives for anyone**. Details and caveats: [Starting a new cycle](/admin/starting-a-new-cycle).

## Stage 3 — Re-cascade OKRs, top down

1. **Owners** set the new [company objectives](/admin/company-objectives) — three to five, ambitious, qualitative.
2. **The CEO** writes their exec OKRs against them.
3. **Managers** run [Setting OKRs for your team](/managers/setting-team-okrs) for every report — **Suggest with AI** is fastest right after company objectives are in, since it grounds on them.
4. **Top-level people** add their own.

## Stage 4 — Verify before the first check-in

- Every person shows OKRs on the Company page (owners can eyeball this in one scroll).
- The **This week** banners show Week 1 of the new cycle.
- Expect dashboards at 0% — they'll move with the first round of check-ins.

<Tip>
  Book the rollover as a calendar ritual: archive on the last Friday, company objectives by Monday, all personal OKRs set before the first check-in due day. One week, start to finish.
</Tip>
